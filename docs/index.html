Political Science · Education Politics · Public Policy

I study how schools shape democracy, and how democracy shapes schools.

I am an Assistant Professor of Political Science at North Central College, where I teach American politics, political behavior, public policy, and education politics. I am also a research affiliate at Brown University’s Annenberg Institute and the Realizing Rights Lab.

I study the politics of K–12 education because public schools are among the last places where Americans of different backgrounds can still govern something together. My work explores when that works, when it doesn’t, and what it means for democracy.

Research areas

Partisanship & school boards

How national partisanship reaches local school board elections, from exit polls in school board races to a national measure of district partisanship.

Administrative ease & democratic access

How the design of public institutions makes participation easier or harder, from the costs of voting in elections to accessing special education information via school districts.

Education federalism

How authority over schooling is divided across levels of government, including state spending priorities and the dissolution of the U.S. Department of Education.
